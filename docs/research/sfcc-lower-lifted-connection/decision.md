At a=0 every competing finite-source/ancestor inequality used to establish the source branch is strict. Continuity therefore supplies some open neighbourhood where those branch identities persist; the inverse function theorem supplies local coordinates there. Neither argument supplies a computed radius for that neighbourhood.

In those coordinates, with g=G+r and positive chamfer coefficient w, the local source is

`max(E, C, w*(E+g), w*(C+g))`.

The four inequalities for its solid boundary yield exactly four physical crease rays: (0,0,-q), (0,-q,0), (-q,0,0), and (-q,-q,q), for q>0.
