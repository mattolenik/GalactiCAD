## What the source geometry establishes

Let E be the native twisted side field, L the competing lathe field, I their
inner-round union, and G the signed groove operand. The inner-round radius is
1.4 mm and the chamfer radius is 0.7 mm. Before activation, the physical crease
satisfies E=I=0, G=-0.7, and L>=1.4. **Activation is L=1.4, not L=0.**
The existing native chart's endpoint is beyond this event.

The admitted finite groove and lathe segments have affine distance in radius
and height. Their two level equations isolate the activation:

| Quantity | Enclosure or result |
| --- | --- |
| Physical radial coordinate | [10.433449951584233, 10.433449951584349] mm |
| Physical height | [-2.7364047346689655, -2.736404734668787] mm |

| Height derivative lower bound | 0.6666666666666651 |
| Transverse (I,G,rho) Jacobian lower bound | 0.5085907399463098 |

The new meridian helper proves exterior position by a noncrossing positive radial
ray or a separating support line, then proves an interior projection and unique
nearest finite segment. It replaces the old sufficient condition that radius
exceed every profile vertex; it does not assume that the inner blend is inactive.
The certificate binds actual source occurrences, operators, frames and carrier
coefficients, and retains finite-side, cap and ancestor dominance checks.

Put z=1.4-L. The outgoing physical crease has

`E = z² / (1.4 + sqrt(1.4²-z²))`, with I=0 and G=-0.7.

The finite native side and original twist give its spatial parameterization.
Its first derivative matches the incoming curve at activation: the ideal crease
is C1 there. The proof covers a bounded outgoing radial depth of 0.2 mm, with
strict branch margins and transversality. It does not establish arbitrary
continuation or a global chart inverse.

The native E=0 carrier instead follows

`I = -z² / (1.4 + sqrt(1.4²+z²)) < 0` for z>0.

Together with groove exterior distance 0.7+I, this gives a bounded *ghost*
family: the physical family lies on a smooth chamfer facet, not a physical crease.
The actual native t=16 endpoint associates to the generated ghost image within
1.36e-12 mm; a separate tiny source box proves its smooth physical neighbourhood.
That does not certify every raw native query after the cut or authorize blanket
raw-tail removal.

Physical and generated coefficient families are bounded separately. The generated
offset/weight need not equal exactly 0.7. For the generated ghost, this constant
error cannot be ignored relative to I arbitrarily close to activation. Universal
physical-facet dominance is asserted only for the physical family; the actual
endpoint has its own strict physical-facet check. The receipts distinguish these
claims. There are 96 interval region checks across incoming, outgoing and ghost
families, their two coefficient variants and 16 subdivisions each. Diagnostic
samples illustrate the results; the interval bounds carry the source argument.
