## A: continuous full-scalar continuation through the cutoffs

A binds the actual immutable native/parent-field/cylinder occurrences and ordered
binary source structure. Its admitted class remains narrow: identity/coaxial
frames, a horizontal selected side of a strictly convex profile, supported finite
primitives and a bounded initial angle interval. It does not claim arbitrary
transformed CSG or the eventual termination of the whole crease.

With cylinder radius R, outer sphere radius Rs and axial center ys, and outer
round radius r, its ideal source curve uses

```
a = r (cos(phi) - 1),       b = r (sin(phi) - 1)
rho = R + a,               q = Rs + b
y = ys + sqrt(q^2 - rho^2)
v = v0z + a/nz,            u = sign * sqrt(rho^2 - v^2)
```

The actual extrusion twist rotates `(u,v)` into world coordinates. The equations
are parameterized source geometry, not a replacement scene evaluator. Existing
field evaluation is used separately to corroborate finite numerical proposals.

The new proof goes beyond zero-locus equivalence. On the complete admitted
interval it checks the selected finite profile edge is the unique nearest edge,
so the signed polygon distance is exactly a even for a<0. Native and cylinder
cap fields stay strictly below a. The inner round-union competitor is strictly
inactive, and both the groove operand and the represented chamfer diagonal stay
below a. The final cap cutter also stays below a. Consequently the complete
native and parent hard operands equal a and the outer sphere field equals b.
The actual round relation `(r+a)^2+(r+b)^2=r^2` then establishes the zero surface.

Locally the full source is the maximum of the two lifted round fields. The lifted
cylinder field has positive radial derivative at least cos(phi), while the lifted
extrusion field has nonzero angular component of magnitude at least
`cos(phi)*abs(u)/rho`. The resulting transverse minor has a positive lower bound.
This establishes two exposed local flanks meeting in a regular continuing crease,
not merely two vectors that happen to differ at sampled points. Positive height
derivative establishes ordered, injective ideal continuation. At phi=0 it joins
the incoming fixed-radius source helix with the same tangent direction.

For the required interval phi in `[0,0.01]`, the executed transition-height bound
is `[2.6957935656967407, 2.6957935656967558]` mm. Height derivative is bounded below
by `1.1295695107473651`, and the transverse minor by `0.6645258866612379`.
All six source margins are strictly positive. This interval continues past both
current numerical upper cutoffs.

The optional phi interval `[0,0.5]` also passes. Its end height is enclosed by
`[3.327511405108021, 3.32751140510805]` mm, with transverse minor lower bound
`0.3309738820352631`; it therefore includes a unique ideal source point at height
3. This does not by itself numerically associate every point of B's compiled trace
with that source curve, or certify the rest of that trace beyond this interval.

At the two current cutoff heights, the *ideal native zero extension* has positive
full-source residual: approximately `3.54372e-7` and `3.45075e-8` respectively.
Thus E=C=0 is no longer the exact outgoing source locus. This is not an assertion
that every approximate point in those numerical bands violates an export error
tolerance; it establishes which source curve must be represented and connected.

The eventual finite-cap, nearest-edge or ancestor transitions remain unproved.
The native finite cap can become active before a nominal sphere-only vanishing
angle endpoint; no such nominal endpoint is accepted as the actual terminal.

## C: ownership is not source closure

C keeps normal feature construction unchanged and applies a scoped binary
ancestor correction only to finite diagnostic queries. It then separates
corrected field values from the existing native-band ownership exclusion.

The actual band is `3.796655370190979e-7`. At source proposals 0.0002, 0.0005 and
0.001 mm above the activation proposal, the actual hard target levels have
magnitudes approximately `1.24e-8`, `7.73e-8` and `3.09e-7`. Corrected lifted
fields satisfy the sampled source/domain conditions when ownership exclusion is
disabled, but the normal band rejects them. Samples at 0.002, 0.01 and 0.1 mm
above activation are outside the band and are admitted. Tested normal gaps exceed
1.15, so these points do not exhibit a vanishing-angle terminal.

A's independent source theorem supplies the continuous geometric premise that
finite C samples alone cannot. In the ideal source, `a=r(cos(phi)-1)` approaches
zero continuously, so a positive band used as a domain exclusion removes an
initial interval of that regular outgoing curve. C does not prove total floating
evaluation on that interval or infer an exported hole: numerical native geometry
may approximate part of the same spatial region within its bands.

Setting the band to zero is not a complete closure contract: the predicate still
rejects an exact zero target. Its numerical activation proposal is not asserted
to be that exact event. Conversely, removing exclusion globally permits lifted
domains in the incoming native-zero region already represented by native curves;
the zero-band numerical counterfactual also exhibits overlap. These are domain
permissions, not a claim that a compiled duplicate edge was produced by C.

The diagnostic therefore rejects global band deletion or a bare tolerance change
as a sufficient repair. Discovery ownership, geometric one-sided closure and
endpoint/connection authority need distinct justified treatment.
